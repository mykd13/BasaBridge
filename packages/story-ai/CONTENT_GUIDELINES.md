# Story content guidelines

Rules for what generated stories are about and how they sound. They are written for Filipino children aged about 6 to 14 who read English as a second or third language. Owner: Jhade (BB-72).

The lists in this file are also in `src/guidelines.ts` as data, so the story prompt and the checks use the same words. Change both together.

## Settings, names and food

- Set stories in places a Filipino child knows: a barangay, a school, a sari-sari store, a market, a farm, a fishing village, a city street, a jeepney or tricycle ride, a fiesta, a family gathering.
- Use Filipino first names (Lia, Ben, Ana, Mateo, Mia, Paolo, Joy, Carlo) and family words (Lola, Lolo, Kuya, Ate, Tita, Tito). Never use the name of a real person.
- Use food and things children see every day: rice, pandesal, adobo, sinigang, mango, banana, calamansi, halo-halo, merienda, a carabao, a bamboo stick.
- Weather and seasons are the Philippine ones: the rainy season, the dry season, typhoon signals, hot afternoons. No snow days or autumn leaves as everyday events.
- Money is pesos. Distances are in metres and kilometres.

## What stories must not have

- **No stereotypes.** No group of people (by region, ethnic group, religion, gender, body or family income) is lazy, poor, funny or dangerous because of who they are. Girls and boys do the same kinds of things. Indigenous peoples and Muslim Filipinos appear as ordinary neighbours, never as exotic.
- **Nothing frightening.** No death, serious injury, violence, weapons, kidnapping, ghosts, monsters or horror. A storm or a lost pet is fine when it ends safely and calmly.
- **No brands.** No company, product, shop chain, app, game, film or celebrity names.
- **No unexplained idioms or culture-bound jokes below level 56.** Say what you mean in plain words. "It was very easy", not "it was a piece of cake". Jokes that need knowledge of another country's TV, holidays or sayings are out at every level.
- **No adult topics.** No romance, politics, elections, religion as a debate, money worries, illness, alcohol, gambling or social media.
- **No lessons that shame.** A character can make a mistake and fix it. Nobody is mocked for being poor, slow, from the province or for how they speak.

## Good topics

- helping at home, cooking with Lola, feeding pets and farm animals
- school days: a class garden, a science project, a reading corner, a sports day
- the market, the sari-sari store, buying pandesal in the morning
- fiestas, birthdays, family reunions, Christmas lights and parol making
- the sea and rivers: fishing, the tide, shells, mangroves
- planting rice, caring for a carabao, a mango tree
- weather: the rainy season, getting ready for a typhoon safely
- crafts and games: kites, sipa, patintero, drawing, music
- helping a neighbour, sharing, fixing a mistake
- nature: birds, insects, fireflies, a nearby mountain or waterfall

## Topics to avoid

- death, serious illness, accidents with injuries
- war, crime, violence, guns, police chases
- ghosts, aswang and other horror creatures
- disasters told for fear (a typhoon is fine as preparation and recovery)
- politics, elections, religion as an argument
- romance and dating
- money worries, debt, job loss
- brands, shops, apps, games, films, celebrities
- holidays and customs a Filipino child would not know (Thanksgiving, Halloween parties, snow days)

## Vocabulary

Most words in a story must come from the allowed list for its tier (see `src/vocab/README.md`). Filipino words from the local list above count as known. A few new words are fine when the sentence around them explains them. The checks count new words; the prompt asks for them to be explained by context.

## Review

A reviewer from the Philippines reads a sample at each tier and records problems. Problems that come back more than once become a prompt change or a new check.
